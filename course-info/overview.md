## **About This Course** 

This course will address what is increasingly the most important concept in Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML).  AI/ML adoption is increasing at a rapid rate and the algorithms and technology in many ways have surpassed human performance.  There is an industry trend of low-code/no-code environments where user interfaces enable AI/ML development by less technically skilled practitioners.  This results in typical opportunities for improvement occurring at the point where humans interact with AI/ML in developing training data, deciding where to apply machine vs human resources, establishing decision policies for AI/ML outputs.  This course will introduce key challenges in holistic AI processes, methods for measuring and improving performance, and design considerations for users.\
\
By the end of this course, you will be able to: 

1. Analyze the principles of human computation, human-AI collaboration, and Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) system design, explaining how human dans AI contribute complementary computational capabilities.
2. Design human-AI systems that appropriately allocate tasks among humans, AI models, and hybrid workflows while incorporating effective human oversight, quality assurance, and governance mechanisms.
3. Evaluate AI systems using empirical methods including human evaluation, crowdsourcing, prompt evaluation, benchmarking, and experimental design to assess system performance, reliability, and robustness.
4. Assess the ethical, societal, and organizational implications of AI deployment, including bias, transparency, explainability, trust, governance, and responsible AI practices.
5. Apply modern AI development techniques (AI-assisted coding, prompt engineering, worklow optimization, and agentic AI design) to create reliable and effective AI-enabled applications.
6. Critically compare the strengths and limitations of humans, crowds, LLMs, and hybrid human-AI systems to determine appropriate computational approaches for diverse real-world problems.
7. Develop and communicate evidence-based recommendations for the design, evaluation, and governance of Human-in-the-Loop AI systems through technical analysis, experimentation, and written reports.
